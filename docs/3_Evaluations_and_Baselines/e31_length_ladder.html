E31 length ladder: 2k → 128k

Same exam at every length: facts hidden in a DNA-style book, question at the end, and after the question the model sees only the memory. 30M model, windowed global read, so the memory is the only long path. Every score here is first-letter accuracy: is the first letter of the answer right? Chance is 25 %. Rows: 64–128 per length, so about ±4 points. Updated 1 Oct 2026, after the E30 vs E31 limits study (E31b).

Which E31 is "best". All variants share the same writer: a two-way page encoder inside each 256-token window, then 32 latents per window, one latent per 6 new tokens. They differ in how a latent's key carries position:
  • Content-only address (e31_li_m1): best pure lookup and recall at length, but slots carry no order, so the in-order chain stays at about 50 %.
  • Order code (e31_ord_m1): slot keys sit before the question at a scaled distance. This is the only variant whose chain transfers in length (3 of 3 seeds), but lookup at 128k drops on 2 of 3 seeds.
  • Mixed (e31_mix_m1): half the key's rotation pairs carry content, half carry order. It sits between the two: lookup 83 % and chain 70 % at 128k.
E30 is parked (1 Oct). Its lines stay dashed for reference.

Lookup: one fact, after the full curriculum (2k → 8k → 16k stages)

Dashed black line = pass mark (75 %); dotted = chance. The first E31 memory, with an absolute window-start address, reaches only about twice its training length: the grey line falls to chance by 16k. The length-invariant address fixes that. Order codes cost some lookup reach at 64k–128k; the 16k stage recovers most of it for the mixed code.

Chain (4 in-order hops): only E31 with an order code transfers

Every chain starts from the model's lookup weights and is trained at 2k (plus one 8k stage where marked). Without an order code, E31 narrows the answer to about two candidates (≈ 50 %). E30 solves the chain only at its training length, by position, in every one of six address variants tried.

The one-read limit. The in-order chain can be solved by "follow the order". The parallel chain (four shuffled chains, start node picks one) needs real hop-by-hop lookup. It stays at chance for every memory and for the dense model at 30M. A weight-tied repeated read (E33 query loop) gave no gain. This is the open problem for latent reasoning.

Many facts: recall 8 or 16 values, with one 8k stage

With the 8k stage both writers carry multi-fact recall to 128k. One reader entry per latent (m1) keeps it. Five entries per latent (m5) dilutes the read at 64k–128k (recall8 falls to 72 %). Decoys (one true fact among 8 look-alikes, 1k training only): E31-m1 89 % at 128k, E30 31 %.

Numbers (first letter, %)

· = not measured. Lookup values for the absolute-address rows and the no-memory control are the mean over answer letters; for single-fact lookup on E31 the mean and the first letter agree (98 / 98).

Honest caveats

  • Takeoff is seed luck at 2k. Content-only m1: 2 of 3 seeds take off on the 2k lookup (one seed never did, so its stages stayed at chance). Order m1: the 128k lookup is 98 % on seed 2 but 47–48 % on seeds 0 and 1. Step size 5e-5 is required.
  • The single best lookup line (94 % at 128k) is one seed. The order-code chain transfer replicates on 3 of 3 seeds (58–75 % at 128k after 2k training).
  • Earlier versions of this page used teacher-forced mean accuracy. On exams with several candidate answers that mean is inflated: the first letters identify the candidate and the rest are copied. The chain claim "82 % at 128k" from 26 Sep was such an inflation and is withdrawn.
  • Training beyond 64k does not fit a 3090 without activation checkpointing, so 128k is evaluation only. 1M runs out of memory for every model; a streaming forward is needed.
