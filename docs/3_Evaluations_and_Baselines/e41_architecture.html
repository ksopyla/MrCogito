Tree Concept Model

One model built around a tree of concepts. The trunk holds a few coarse summaries of the whole text, the branches hold finer ones, and the leaves are the tokens themselves. A reader walks down from the trunk, rereading as it goes, and a thin decoder writes the next word from what the walk found.

Your two additions are built in: the next-concept loss and the NCM-style decoder. Everything on this page is my reading of your idea, drawn so you can correct it before I write the spec.

One rule holds it together: predict, then keep what the prediction missed.

Down the treeA summary predicts the finer summaries beneath it. Each child stores only its difference from the parent. This is NCM's ladder.
Along the textThe reasoner predicts the next concept before the text arrives, and training scores the guess. This is the next-concept loss.
At the leavesWhat no summary can predict, a name or a number, stays in the text itself and is reached by position, word for word.

  • Rereading steers. From the trunk, the fact and its look-alike seem equally good. After two rereads the question is sharper and the look-alike branch is let go. Today's model already loops four times over its read, so each pass can go about two levels deeper.

Part 2

The whole model

Four parts. Three run on every token, one runs on concepts only. Step through them.

A first split of today's budget of about 141M weights, all 896 wide: writer 4 layers, reader 2 layers looped four times, decoder 3 layers, reasoner 3 layers. Today's concept memory spends all 13 layers on tokens, and its concept reasoner is switched off in the release.

Part 3

What one concept holds

A concept at any level, from a twig of 8 tokens to a trunk concept of 2M, keeps the same three things. This is your sentence about concepts and the indices of specific tokens, made concrete.

A summary

How many tokens lie beneath. A key with its spread, used to estimate how much attention the whole branch deserves. A value: what the branch says, stored as its difference from the parent. A working state of 896 numbers that the reasoner reads and rewrites.

Pointers down

To its eight children. A twig points at its member tokens by position, so they can be opened word for word. Each concept also keeps a short list of stand-out tokens beneath it: the ones its summaries predict worst, such as names and numbers.

A code

A label from a codebook, as in NCM. It is the target for next-concept prediction and the form the plan takes. Reading stays on the continuous summaries, because a code alone carries too little: one of 4,096 is 12 bits.

What "learned" means here. Three things get trained that are fixed formulas today: who groups with whom, what the summary says, and where the reader goes. The plain average stays only as the starting point of the summary, with a learned correction on top that begins at zero. H-Net's ablations found that the squeezing formula matters little; the network in front of it and the pressure from training matter. So most of the work is in Part 4.

Part 4

How training forms the summaries

Today nothing forces a summary to be good. The best 512 tokens are always reopened, so the loss is low without the summaries holding anything. Measured on 64k documents, the summaries alone score 0.091 nats per token worse than cutting the document to its last 1k tokens (lower is better), while the far past with rereads is worth 0.142. The tree model trains with six signals. Five come from NCM.

kept from today

Next word

The usual loss, with the far past read through the tree. It stays the anchor: in the text work we reviewed, predicting concepts without it collapsed or drifted to the average.

NCM: nested dropout

Closed-book cuts

On half the rows the reader is stopped at a random depth: trunk only, down to the branches, or down to the twigs with nothing opened. The loss can then fall only through the summaries of that level. E37f, specified today, tests the one-level version on today's model.

NCM: the tokenizer's reconstruction

Rebuild the passage

On some rows the decoder is handed a passage's own summaries, cut at a random depth, and must write that passage. Every passage gives signal on every step, whether or not a later question needs it. It also measures compression directly: bits saved per token with one summary per 512 tokens, per 64, per 8.

NCM: the generator's loss

Next concept

Before a passage is read, the reasoner predicts its codes, coarsest level first. The guess is scored against what the writer then writes. This trains the reasoner and pulls summaries toward the gist, the part that can be predicted. Facts cannot be predicted, which is why they stay on the exact path.

NCM: the residual ladder

Parent predicts children

For real questions from the batch, a parent's estimate of how much attention its branch deserves must match what its children say. The child keeps only the difference. This is what sends the walk down the right branch.

NCM: code corruption

Wrong notes on purpose

Some branches are swapped for branches of another document in the batch, and the decoder sometimes gets a noisy plan. The reader learns to check a summary by going deeper. Swapped branches also make a 64k row look like a far longer text at almost no cost.

A 64k row. The proportions are a first guess: half the rows as normal, half cut.

Outside evidence for the next-concept loss. Added to next-word training, it reached the same loss with about half the tokens in one 2026 preprint (NCP, read from the abstract only) and with 21.5% fewer in CoCoMix, which also feeds the predicted concept back into the model, as the plan does here. Large Concept Models found that predicting concepts by squared error drifts to the average, so the target here is a code scored by cross-entropy.

Part 7

Where it could break

  • One fact seen from the trunk. A single summary of 2M tokens cannot show that one password lies beneath it. This is the risk I rate highest. The defences are the stand-out token lists, a wide walk (16 or more followed per level, best first), and the parent-predicts-children signal. If they are not enough, the fallback is to scan all twig summaries at lengths where that is affordable and use the tree only above.
  • Many new parts at once. Going all in means a failure will not name its cause. Each part gets an off switch for testing and its own readout: closed-book value per level, next-concept accuracy per level, rebuild bits per level, how often the walk reaches the right twig.
  • The plan may not help the next word. A plan for a 512-token passage adds little to a decoder that already sees the last 512 to 1,023 tokens. The plan's input starts at zero, so it cannot hurt, and the next-concept loss still shapes the summaries.
  • Fewer token layers. Three layers move from tokens to concepts. Plain language quality at short range could drop. The no-harm checks against concept memory decide.
  • Speed is unmeasured. The read gets smaller and the reasoner is cheap (it runs on one position per seven tokens, so twelve passes cost less than two token layers), but the walk has data-dependent steps that may not compile as well as today's read.
Part 8

Five choices before the spec

Each one changes what gets built. My pick is marked; tell me where you see it differently.

How twigs are cut

Today's grouping by meaning inside 512-token blocks, proven in concept memory. Or passages cut where the meaning changes, as in living concepts, whose rerun reports on 27 Nov. With blocks, "the next concept" has an order only from the 512 level up; inside a passage the reasoner predicts an unordered set of codes.

Pick: blocks now. The tree above the twigs is the same either way.

Children per concept

8 gives seven levels at 10M. 4 gives twelve, as many as NCM uses. 64 gives three. Fewer, wider levels leave fewer chances for a wrong turn; more levels give finer closed-book cuts.

Pick: 8.

Where the weights go

All in: 4 writer, 2 looped reader, 3 decoder and 3 reasoner layers. Or keep today's body and swap only the memory, which could start from the released weights.

Pick: all in, as you said. It means training from scratch.

The first run

From scratch at 150M on the same data as the recall run, compared with it and with concept memory. Earlier from-scratch 150M runs cost about $450; this one depends on its speed. A short pipeline check at home comes first.

Pick: one full run, no smaller screen.

The plan in run one

The decoder gets the plan from the first step, through an input that starts at zero. Or the next-concept loss acts only as a training signal in the first run and the plan joins later.

Pick: plan in from the start.
