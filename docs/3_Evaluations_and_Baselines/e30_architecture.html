How information flows through the sliding-window notebook

Same backbone as the full read and the averaged notebook: a local layer, one global layer, then two more local layers. The new part is the write inside that global layer. Each overlapping window is read by a few learned questions. A note is a weighted pick of the tokens in its own window. Notes do not talk to each other. The answer may look the notes up. It may not reread the raw book.

What this picture is, and is not

  • The tiny book is a teaching row. It was not trained. The 50M row is the in-order 4-hop chain that passed (79% of answer tokens) with a 256-token window and 160 notes.
  • Write reach is the window. At 50M that is 256 tokens. The notebook still covers the whole book because five windows tile it, overlapping by a quarter.
  • There is no mixer between notes. Four hops can combine only in the single global layer, when the answer looks the notes up.
  • The two layers under that are local (16 tokens) and they are cut at the question, so they cannot walk back into the book.
  • The averaged notebook on the same read would store one mean per 16 tokens (64 notes at length 1024, each seeing 16). This picture is the learned-question write instead.
