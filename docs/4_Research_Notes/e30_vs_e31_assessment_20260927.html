Correction, 28 Sep: read before the scores.

Answer accuracy in this page is the teacher-forced mean over 32 answer letters. When the book holds several candidate answers, the first one or two given letters identify the candidate and the rest are copied. That inflates every chain, lookalike, shuffled and multi-fact score. The honest score is first-letter accuracy. Re-measured:

  • 4-hop chain. E31 (and E31-LI) is at 50–59 % first-letter at 2k (mean 96–98 %): it narrows to about 2 candidates but does not follow the hops. "Chain 82 % at 128k" is withdrawn. E30-LI solves the chain only at its training length, by position.
  • Real multi-hop (chain hidden among decoy chains) is at chance for both. The suite's shuffled-1k cell also had a shortcut: the answer is the only pure target node.
  • Retrieval survives:
    • lookup: one value per book, so the mean is honest;
    • recall of 8 facts: E30-LI 84 % first-letter at 128k;
    • 8 decoys: E31-LI with one reader entry per latent, 89 % at 128k.

Scores below for V3 (multi-hop) are corrected. Everything else is left as first written; see docs/experiments_specs/ahead/E31b_e30_vs_e31_limits.md for the full study.

1 · The short answer

Better at ≤ 2k tokens?
E30_ctx
E30's writer with a 64-token context: 99 % on lookalike-1k and lookup-2k, against 94 % and 89 % for E31. Its writer has about 35× fewer parameters (0.13 M vs 4.5 M). measured
Better at long inputs?
E31 + length-invariant address
80–81 % at 128k (2 seeds, trained on books of at most 16k). E30_ctx and E31's original address fall to chance by 16k. measured
More efficient at 1M?
E30 (N/6)
E31's fine memory keeps 0.83 entries per token, almost as many as full attention. E30 keeps 1 per 6 tokens: 5× smaller, and 0.33 vs 0.45 s per 128k row. measured
Fulfils the vision?
Neither, yet
Nothing we trained compresses beyond about 6 tokens per memory entry. The compression setting (48 tokens per latent) did not learn. What works is a linear-cost, exclusive, length-invariant index, not yet a concept space.
One sentence: E30's real flaw was its 16-token context, not its writer. Given 64 tokens it matches or beats E31 up to 2k at a fraction of the cost. E31's lasting contributions are two:
  • the length-invariant address, a read-side change that could be moved onto E30;
  • a decoupled, stateful writer, the right base for a hierarchy.
The 1M design should combine the two: fine E30_ctx-style slots and coarse E31-style latents over them, with length-invariant addressing and a top-k fetch driven by the question.

3 · Criteria that follow from the vision

Vision: a small, efficient reasoning model with a 1M–10M-token context. It compresses long input into latent concepts, reasons over them, and answers. Each criterion below is something that vision needs; the weights say how much.

5 · The 1M / 10M maths

Context N Model Use

Assumptions (so you can check the maths)

  • Memory entries. Our platform has 1 key/value head of 64 dims, so one entry is K + V = 2 × 64 × 2 bytes = 256 B per reading layer. The 1B-class preset uses 8 KV heads × 128 = 4 KB, with 24 full layers for dense.
  • Reading cost = R reading tokens × reading layers × entries × 4 × d_model FLOPs (QKᵀ and AV). Dense reads N keys in every layer.
  • Prefill (ingest).
    • Dense: 2·P·N + L · 4 · d · N²/2.
    • Memory models: 2·P·N + the writer: ≈ 10 MFLOP per token for E31 (≈ 15 % of the 62 MFLOP main path), ≈ 1 MFLOP for E30.
    • For the 30M platform, times are measured at 128k and extrapolated: dense 2.29 s × (N/128k)², E31 0.45 s, E30_ctx 0.33 s, local-only 0.29 s × N/128k.
  • Two-level (proposed): a coarse index of N/48 entries is read densely. The question then fetches the top 16 pages and reads their fine entries: 16 pages × 64 entries per 384-token page at N/6. Three-level adds an N/384 level above the coarse one.
  • The fine store of the two-level design (N/6) can live in CPU memory; only the coarse index sits on the GPU.

6 · Measured cost per row vs length (one RTX 3090, evaluation)

From the ladder logs. At 2k–8k rows are batched with sdpa, so the per-row time is amortised. From 16k on it is flex with batch 1–4. At 128k:

  • dense 2.29 s per row (quadratic: ×3.4 per doubling);
  • E31 0.45 s, E30_ctx 0.33 s, local-only 0.29 s (linear).
Peak GPU memory is 3.3–3.4 GB for every architecture at 128k, dominated by the main path's activations, not the memory. The current implementation therefore needs about 27 GB at 1M and would not fit a 3090 in one pass. A streaming writer, window by window and keeping only the slots, fixes that: slots are 43 MB for E30 and 213 MB for E31 at 1M.
Correction: I earlier quoted 1.35 s per 128k row. That smoke test ran on a GPU shared with a training job; the ladder figure above is the reliable one.

7 · Where E30 is better, and where E31 is better

E30_ctx is better at …

  • Exact recall up to 2k: lookalike-1k 99.3 % vs 94 %; lookup-2k 99.0 % vs 89.2 % (3-seed medians). measured
  • Memory size: N/6 entries vs 0.83 N, 5× smaller. At 1M on our width that is 43 MB vs 213 MB. measured geometry
  • Speed: 0.33 vs 0.45 s per 128k row. Its writer adds 12 % over local-only; E31's adds 55 %. measured
  • Robustness at 2k: lookup-2k seeds 84 / 99 / 99 %, against 98 / 89 / 47 % for E31. measured
  • Simplicity: +0.1 M params instead of +4.5 M (E31 is 14 % bigger than the controls, beyond the suite's ±5 %). measured
  • Verbatim fidelity: a slot is a weighted mix of real token keys and values, so copying exact content is natural. design

E31 is better at …

  • Length: with the length-invariant address it reaches 80–81 % at 128k and chain 82 % at 128k. E30_ctx reaches 29 % at 16k. measured, but the address was never tried on E30 untested.
  • Multi-hop: chain-2k 98.2 % vs 97.4 %, shuffled-1k 98 % vs 96.5 %. A small margin, about 1 SE. measured
  • Language-like noise: Glyph fact-1k 99 % vs 86 % for E30 as built. E30_ctx was not run on Glyph. measured / untested
  • Decoupled writer: it reads token embeddings with its own weights, independent of the language model. Memory can be precomputed, cached and shared, and the question-side model can change without rewriting it. design
  • Stateful latents: 512-d state with a feed-forward block, 8 heads each making its own pick, and competition. These can be pooled into higher levels, the route to a concept hierarchy. design

11 · Verdict and next steps

Blunt verdict

  • Is E30 better than E31? E30 as built is not; it is worse everywhere except cost. E30_ctx is better at 1–2k, 5× smaller and cheaper. It is worse at length, but only because it still has the absolute address.
  • More efficient at 1M: E30 (N/6). E31's fine memory (0.83 N) is about the size of full attention's keys.
    • For the "read once, decide" use, both are linear: about 40–50× faster prefill than dense at 1M and about 400× at 10M on our platform.
    • For a reasoning model that reads memory at every generated token, a flat memory still grows linearly per read, and E31's is 5× more expensive.
  • At 30M, compute is not the wall at 1M. Dense prefill is about 2 minutes. The walls are:
    • generalising beyond the training length (dense and absolute addresses fail at 2×);
    • quadratic prefill at 10M (about 3.7 h for dense);
    • memory at real widths (a 1B-class dense model needs about 96 GB of KV cache at 1M).
  • The vision: "compress into concepts, reason in concept space" is not shown by either. What is shown is a trainable, exclusive, linear-cost memory channel. It retrieves exact facts (it does not follow multi-hop chains; see the correction), and with the length-invariant address it works 8–16× beyond its training length. That is the retrieval half of the vision. The compression half (≥ 48 tokens per unit) failed.
  • Potential: E31's pieces (decoupled stateful writer, length-invariant address) are the better base for a hierarchy. E30_ctx is the better fine store. Keep both, in two levels.
