# E22 root cause — the concept array was used as local depth, not as memory

**Date:** 2026-09-12 · **Kind:** dated root-cause diagnosis (append-only ledger entry)
**Spec:** [E22](../experiments_specs/done_failed/E22_perceiver_concept_lm.md) · plan
[E22_plan](../experiments_specs/done_failed/E22_perceiver_concept_lm_plan.md) · run report
[e22_pilot_verdict_20260912](../2_Experiments_Registry/run_reports/e22_pilot_verdict_20260912.md)
**Prior analysis:** [Perceiver revisit synthesis](perceiver_revisit_synthesis_20260912.md) (the three laws E22 was built on) ·
[E18 family verdict](../2_Experiments_Registry/run_reports/e18_family_verdict_20260912.md)
**Code:** `nn/perceiver_concept_lm.py` at `8914029` (as run) → `0f4b4f9` (this note's instrument: `near`/`far` ablations, `concept_xattn_scope`)

> **Verdict.** E22 built the first ledger model whose decoder has *no raw route* past its 1024-token
> segment, and the concept array is demonstrably load-bearing (Δ_none ≈ 0.22–0.27 nats, > 10σ). But the
> array is load-bearing **everywhere equally, including inside the first segment where no long-range slot
> exists**, a segment-only 8-layer decoder (arm C, half the compute) reaches the same loss, and reading
> *another document's* array hurts less than reading none at 16k–32k. A `near`/`far` ablation added after
> the run decomposes the 0.25 nats: **≈ 0.05 is the marginal value of the far slots** (the long-range memory
> the bet was about — flat from 1k to 32k, 6× under the gate), ≈ 0.03 is the marginal of the token's
> own-segment slots (a local bypass the `cpos ≤ pos` mask allowed), and ≈ 0.17 is document-level information
> present redundantly in every slot — a *document embedding*, available from near or far slots alike, and
> recoverable by arm C from its raw segment. The objective never paid for far content (CE on natural text is
> worth ≈ 0.05 nats beyond 1k at this scale), and the mask never forced the array to be the only route for
> anything. Two laws follow; E23 is built on them.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 Loss: the array adds nothing a segment-only decoder does not have (S2 ❌, K1 ✓ triggered)

| step | A eval | C eval | dense eval |
|---|---|---|---|
| 320 | 4.691 | 4.682 | **4.616** (−1.6%) |
| 560 | 4.167 | 4.172 | — (lost) |

Train loss 4.16 for both A and C at 560. Position buckets on 64 held-out PG-19 rows ≥ 32k, teacher-forced:

| bucket | A `real` | C | A − C |
|---|---|---|---|
| [0, 1k) | 4.249 | 4.195 | **+0.054** |
| [1k, 4k) | 4.204 | 4.210 | −0.006 |
| [4k, 8k) | 4.210 | 4.215 | −0.005 |
| [8k, 16k) | 4.151 | 4.155 | −0.004 |
| [16k, 32k) | 4.162 | **4.133** | **+0.029** |

S2 asked for A ≤ 0.97 × C on positions ≥ 4k; we got A ≈ 1.00 × C and A *worse* at 16k–32k. K1 ("at 40% of budget A not below C on far positions") was met exactly. Arm A spends 2× the compute of arm C for the same loss: the encoder + pooler + latent stack (65M compute parameters) contributed **nothing** to the loss that the 8-layer decoder could not do alone.

### 2.2 Concept ablation: load-bearing, but distance-blind (S1 ❌ on the content half)

Arm A, `--probe concept`, 64 rows, Δ = CE(mode) − CE(real), mean ± se over rows:

| bucket | Δ_none | Δ_shuffled | shuffled − none | Δ_none − Δ_none[0,1k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [0, 1k) | 0.218 ± 0.017 | 0.260 ± 0.028 | +0.042 | — |
| [1k, 4k) | 0.268 ± 0.016 | 0.318 ± 0.029 | +0.050 | +0.050 |
| [4k, 8k) | 0.257 ± 0.009 | 0.294 ± 0.023 | +0.037 | +0.039 |
| [8k, 16k) | 0.250 ± 0.008 | 0.264 ± 0.022 | +0.014 | +0.032 |
| [16k, 32k) | 0.232 ± 0.008 | 0.191 ± 0.014 | **−0.040** | +0.014 |

Three readings, each decisive on its own:

1. **The first segment is a built-in control.** A token at position p < 1024 can only see slots whose block ends ≤ p — all inside its own raw segment. There is no long-range slot to lose. Yet Δ_none = 0.218 there. That number is the pure "array as extra local capacity" effect. In every later bucket, Δ_none exceeds it by only 0.014–0.050 nats — the **upper bound on what far slots contribute**, and it *shrinks* with distance. S1 wanted ≥ 0.30 from far history; the far channel delivers ≤ 0.05.
2. **Wrong-document concepts beat no concepts at 16k–32k.** `shuffled` keeps every slot position and replaces the content with another PG-19 book. Beyond 16k that costs 0.04 nats *less* than removing the array. Whatever the decoder extracts from distant slots is generic — register, "long-book mode", position-conditioned statistics — not the content of *this* document. Near range (< 4k) has the opposite sign: same-segment slots carry local specifics, so foreign ones mislead.
3. **Arm C recovers the 0.22 on its own.** Arm A with the array removed sits at 4.47; arm C, the same 8-layer decoder trained without an array, sits at 4.19. The decoder in A offloaded ~0.22 nats of modelling that an 8-layer decoder can do from its raw segment into the encoder→array path. The mask made that the cheapest gradient route: a 6-layer-encoder + 4-layer-latent view of the *same* tokens the decoder already sees raw — a deeper view than the decoder's own early layers, delivered one slot per 16 tokens, and (because `cpos ≤ pos` includes the block that ends *at* p) containing a 10-layer-deep representation of the current token itself for 1 in 16 positions.

### 2.3 Retrieval through the array: none (S4 ❌)

passkey @8k / @32k = 0.000 for A and C; multikey, vt 0.000; keyed-recall first-token 4.8% (A) vs 3.5% (C — structurally cannot retrieve, so 3.5% is the floor). E18b-R 4.2%, E18b dense 99.3%. lm-eval 0-shot avg 0.298 (A) vs 0.305 (C) — chance for both at this scale, no signal.

### 2.4 Direct test — `near` vs `far` ablation (added 2026-09-12 evening)

Commit `0f4b4f9` adds two eval-time modes: `near` = only slots whose block ends inside the token's raw
segment stay visible (the far slots are removed); `far` = only slots ending before the segment stay
visible (the near slots are removed). Same 64 rows, arm A final, `Cache/eval/e22_armA/concept_nearfar.json`.
Prediction from §2.2: removing the far slots costs ≈ 0; the first segment must show Δ_near = 0 exactly
(nothing to remove) and Δ_far = Δ_none (only the inert null slot remains).

| bucket | CE real | CE none | CE `near` (far slots removed) | CE `far` (near slots removed) | **marginal of far slots** = Δ(`near`) | **marginal of near slots** = Δ(`far`) | Δ_none | sum of marginals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [0, 1k) | 4.249 | 4.467 | 4.249 | 4.468 | 0.000 ± 0.000 | 0.220 ± 0.018 | 0.218 | (control ✓) |
| [1k, 4k) | 4.204 | 4.472 | 4.258 | 4.229 | **0.054 ± 0.002** | 0.026 ± 0.004 | 0.268 | 0.080 |
| [4k, 8k) | 4.210 | 4.467 | 4.268 | 4.233 | **0.057 ± 0.002** | 0.022 ± 0.003 | 0.257 | 0.079 |
| [8k, 16k) | 4.151 | 4.401 | 4.210 | 4.169 | **0.059 ± 0.002** | 0.018 ± 0.002 | 0.250 | 0.077 |
| [16k, 32k) | 4.162 | 4.393 | 4.208 | 4.192 | **0.046 ± 0.004** | 0.030 ± 0.003 | 0.232 | 0.076 |

(Per-token tails: removing the near slots changes > 0.1 nats on 21% of tokens, removing the far slots on
33%, removing everything on 51%.)

Reading:
- **Control passes.** Segment 0: removing the far slots costs 0.000 (there are none), removing the near
  slots costs 0.220 = Δ_none (only the inert null slot remains). The instrument measures what it claims to.
- **The far slots' marginal value is ≈ 0.05 nats, flat in distance** (0.054 / 0.057 / 0.059 / 0.046 from 1k
  to 32k). That is the long-range memory the bet was about — **6× under the 0.30 gate**, with no growth
  when 8× more history is available. It matches the upper bound from §2.2 (1) exactly.
- **The near slots' marginal value is ≈ 0.02–0.03 nats.** The local bypass through the mask exists but is the
  smaller term; it confounds the S1 measurement more than it explains the loss.
- **Strong sub-additivity: 0.05 + 0.03 ≈ 0.08 ≪ 0.25.** About 0.17 nats is information that near and far
  slots supply *interchangeably*. It is not residual-stream calibration (then `shuffled`, which keeps every
  slot's magnitude, would beat `none` everywhere; it loses to `none` below 16k). It is **document-level
  content redundantly present in every slot** — vocabulary, register, characters, the "which book is this"
  embedding — which arm C's decoder recovers from its own 1024 raw tokens (arm C is *better* than A in
  [0, 1k): 4.195 vs 4.249). Beyond 16k, a wrong book's embedding is closer to the right prior than nothing,
  hence `shuffled` < `none` there.
- So the array's 0.25 nats decompose as ≈ 0.17 **document embedding** (distance-blind, redundant across
  slots, cheaply available from raw text) + ≈ 0.05 **far-specific content** (the memory) + ≈ 0.03
  **own-segment content** (the bypass). Δ_none as an S1 metric over-states channel value by ≈ 5×; the
  gate should have been the far marginal.

## 3. Root causes (in order of evidence)

**RC1 — Next-token CE on natural text does not pay for far context at this scale.** The far slots'
marginal value is 0.05 nats and flat from 1k to 32k (§2.4); arm C ≈ arm A on far tokens. The CE-reducible
information in tokens > 1024 back, for a ~130M model at 0.44B tokens on PG-19/FinePDFs, is ≈ 0.05 nats —
and the array *did* capture roughly that much. The E18 law ("the read is used but not useful") re-appears on
a *structurally closed* model, which rules out the raw-bypass explanation E18 still admitted. Consequence:
S1's 0.30-nat gate was **unreachable by construction** — no channel can carry more than the objective is
willing to pay for. The 5% keyed-recall share (E18b protocol) did not change the gradient budget: 4.8%
first-token accuracy vs a 3.5% floor. Evidence: §2.1, §2.4.

**RC2 — Incomplete closure: the mask left a *local bypass around the raw path* and made Δ_none a bad
gate.** The spec enforced "every dependency longer than one segment has exactly one route (the array)". It did
not enforce the converse: dependencies *shorter* than one segment had two routes — raw self-attention and
up to 63 same-segment slots — and the slot route is deeper (6 encoder + 4 latent layers). The measured
cost is modest (near marginal 0.03 nats), but it means the array is *never* the only route for anything,
the 0.17-nat document embedding it carries is available to the decoder from raw text anyway, and Δ_none
(0.25) over-states channel value 5× relative to the far marginal (0.05). The bypass law from the revisit
synthesis was applied in one direction only. Evidence: §2.2 (1), (3), §2.4.

**RC3 — (refuted as a slot-representation failure; stands as a *read* failure).** The natural suspicion
was that mean-pooled 16-token slots are near-copies of each other, so the array could only ever carry a
document embedding. Measured on arm A (`model.concepts()`, 8 PG-19 rows at 32k, C = 2048 slots each):

| per-row RankMe of z (centred / raw) | adjacent-slot cosine (mean / p10) | cosine at 8k-token separation | mean pairwise cosine | pooler `wo` ‖W‖_F (init 0) | `wk` ‖W‖_F | null slot ‖·‖ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **265** / 215 of 768 | 0.69 / 0.44 | 0.57 | 0.56 | **11.2** | 9.1 | 0.16 |

S5 (RankMe ≥ 64) **passes by 4×**; no dead slots (norms constant after `concept_norm`, CV = 0); the
learned-query term left zero and is as large as the key projection; adjacent slots are only slightly more
alike than arbitrary pairs (0.69 vs 0.56). The array is *diverse*: roughly half of each unit slot's energy
is a shared document direction (pairwise cosine 0.56), the other half is slot-specific content. The decoder
reads the shared half (≈ 0.17 nats) and almost none of the specific half (≈ 0.05). **The content is in the
array; the read was never trained to address it.** That is RC1 again, and it is good news for E23: the
encoder + pooler + latent stack can be kept, the objective must change. E18b's "keys must be discriminative"
lesson applies to the *cross-attention keys the decoder forms*, not to the slots themselves.

**RC4 — Budget and maturity (multiplier, not cause; weaker than first thought).** The evaluated finals sit
at 560 steps × 24 packed 32k rows ≈ **0.44B tokens — 88% of the 0.5B pilot budget** (an earlier draft of
this note said 0.28B / 56%, using the spec's planned 0.5M tokens/step instead of the launcher's 0.79M);
no dense control past step 320. E16b/E18 showed long-range effects only after ~1B tokens, so more tokens
could change the *size* of the far contribution, but the A ≈ C curves were flat-parallel through the whole
run and the far marginal shows no growth with distance — the budget does not change the sign.

**RC5 — Protocol.** (a) The dense control (S3) was lost to a disk-full crash plus a cleanup-script bug;
S3 is unmeasured. (b) The gates were set on natural-text CE where far information is cheap (RC2). (c) The
first-segment control (§2.2 (1)) was in the data but not in the gates — the spec's "noise-floor check" only
covered positions < r. (d) Arm A′ on Polonez duplicated arm A instead of running a distinct arm.

## 4. What the result does *not* say

- Not that positional concept arrays cannot carry content: E18 P2 retrieved by position at 99.9998% when
  the task paid for it, and E18b's dense control learned the recall task in 0.1B tokens.
- Not that the latent transformer over slots is useless: K > 1 and any long-range supervision were never
  tested. The stack was simply never asked a long-range question.
- Not a dead channel and not a collapsed array: Δ_none is > 10σ in every bucket, gradients flow, arm A
  trained stably (K3 not triggered), RankMe 265 with a live learned-query pooler (§3 RC3). E10–E17 failed
  with *dead* writes and E05 with *collapsed* latents; E22 failed with a *live, diverse array that the
  decoder was never asked to read for content*. That is a better failure — it isolates the objective and
  the mask, not optimisation or representation.

## 5. Conclusions — two laws to add to the three

The revisit synthesis derived three laws (no raw bypass, positional allocation, contextualise before
pooling). E22 satisfied all three and still produced a channel that carries no far content. Two more:

- **Law 4 — The objective must pay for the channel.** On natural text at ≤ 1B tokens, far context is worth
  ≈ 0.05 nats to a 130M model, and a CE-trained array captures about that much and no more; a gate of 0.30
  there is unfalsifiable. Long-range channels must be trained and gated on tokens whose targets are
  *determined* by far content: keyed recall, far copy, multi-hop variable tracking — and on natural text,
  the tokens that are copyable only from far context (far-repeat tokens). The share of such tokens in the
  loss is a first-class knob, not a 5% garnish. Gate on the **far marginal** (`near`/`far` ablation), never on Δ_none.
- **Law 5 — Exclusivity is two-sided.** A concept channel must be the only route for the information it is
  supposed to carry **and must carry nothing the raw path already has**. One-sided closure (E22) lets CE
  use the channel for local depth and document-level priors the decoder can get raw; the opposite one-sided
  closure (E05/E18: raw bypass) lets CE ignore it. Implementation: cross-attention admits only slots whose
  block ends *before* the token's raw window (`concept_xattn_scope=exclusive`, now in the foundation). It is
  worth ≈ 0.03 nats of loss at most and buys a clean measurement and a clean gradient.

**For the 1M / 10M reasoning and agent-to-agent goal.** The agent-to-agent setting *is* Law 5 by
construction: the receiver has no raw access to the sender's context, the message is the only route.
E22 shows what a CE-trained array carries when nothing forces otherwise: a document embedding (0.17 nats
of register, vocabulary, "which book") plus 0.05 of facts. That is a fine *summary* channel and a useless
*memory* channel — an agent receiving it would know what the other agent was reading about, not what it
read. The channel has to be trained on tasks where the message is worth nats: recall, copy, multi-hop —
the same tasks a reasoning core needs from its context. That makes E23's objective the honest next step on
the Vision's path, not a detour. The 96 B/token array cost is only worth paying at 1M–10M once the far
marginal is measured in whole nats on those tasks.

## 6. Fixes and new experiments

**Implemented (commit `0f4b4f9`, config-selectable, old checkpoints unchanged):**
- `concept_xattn_scope = causal | exclusive` on `PerceiverConceptConfig` (+ `--pcl_concept_xattn_scope`,
  `PCL_CONCEPT_XATTN_SCOPE`), shared by the flex and SDPA paths via `raw_span()`; works for
  `dec_local ∈ {block, swa}`.
- `concept_override ∈ {real, none, shuffled, near, far}`; `--probe concept` scores all five by default.
  `far` on an E22 checkpoint equals the exclusive-scope forward with the same weights (tested).

**Proposed — one coherent bet, E23 ([spec](../experiments_specs/ahead/E23_exclusive_concept_channel.md)):**
same platform, exclusive scope (Law 5), and an objective that pays for far content (Law 4): natural-text
CE with a ×8 weight on *far-repeat tokens* (tokens whose 4-gram context recurs ≥ one segment back and not
inside the raw window — the information that genuinely lives in far context) plus 30% dense-label
long-range rows (keyed recall, far copy, multi-hop vt). Gates on recall/passkey and on the **far marginal**
(mode `far` is the model's own forward under exclusive scope, so the instrument becomes `real` vs `none`
vs `shuffled` with segment 0 as the built-in zero). Post-signal knobs: K ∈ {2, 4}, r ∈ {8, 32}, hierarchical
r = 16 / 256, receiver-only decoding (the message probe). Kill: keyed recall ≤ 10% at 60% budget with the
dense control ≥ 90% → a segment-local query cannot content-address a diverse positional array through
one cross-attention; change the *read* (a two-stage read: query the latent stack first, then the slots;
or per-slot K/V of block-end states) before touching the encoder or pooler, which §3 RC3 clears.

**Cheap zero-training checks (no GPU-days) — all done 2026-09-12 evening, Odra:**
1. §2.4 `near`/`far` on arm A — far marginal 0.05, near marginal 0.03, redundant 0.17.
2. S5 geometry on arm A's `z` — RankMe 265/768, adjacent cosine 0.69, pairwise 0.56: diverse, not collapsed (RC3).
3. `pooler.wo` ‖W‖_F = 11.2 (init 0) — the learned-query pooling term is alive and comparable to `wk` (9.1).
Conclusion of the three: keep the encoder / pooler / latent stack for E23; change the mask and the objective.

**Not proposed (and why):** r / c / K sweeps on the E22 objective (registered as post-signal only, and
the signal did not come); `dec_local=swa` ablation (asks about the raw bypass, which E22 already closed);
warm-starting E23 from E22 (its decoder is trained to read near slots — the wrong prior).

## 7. Addendum (same day, after KS asked whether the measures and expectations were right)

Two checks that change the *verdict framing*, not the verdict.

### 7.1 The S1 gate was set ~3× above the physical ceiling — and we had the ceiling in-house

[E18's reach ablation](../2_Experiments_Registry/run_reports/e18_reach_ablation_20260909.md) (2026-09-09,
three days before E22 launched) measured the same quantity on a **dense** transformer of the same width
and scale, by capping every full layer's window with shared weights:

| what | Δ nats (paired) |
|---|---|
| dense control, keys 2k–8k back (window 2048), 64 mixed rows | **+0.035 ± 0.003** |
| dense control, reach 512 → 8k, 64 mixed rows | **+0.105 ± 0.005** |
| dense control, keys 2k–8k back, 8 PG-19 rows @32k | **+0.099 ± 0.017** |
| dense control, keys > 8k back at [16k,32k) | **−0.093 ± 0.027** (RoPE extrapolation *hurts*) |
| **E22 arm A, all slots before the current 1024-token segment** | **+0.046 … +0.059** |

So a dense model with unrestricted raw access extracts 0.035–0.10 nats from far context at this scale and
data, and *negative* value beyond its training length. E22's concept array extracts **0.05** — about half
of the dense PG-19 figure and above the mixed-row figure. **The channel is not weak; the prize is small.**
S1 asked for 0.30 nats, i.e. ~3× more than a dense transformer gets from the same tokens: **no architecture
could have passed it**, and the number that said so was already in our own ledger. Corrected reading of
§3 RC1: the objective does not pay for far content — for *any* reader, not just for concepts — and the
error was ours for gating on an absolute nat count without first measuring its ceiling. (Process fix
landed in the `experiment-design` skill: every absolute gate must cite the control that measured its
ceiling.) This does not rescue E22 — arm C still matched arm A and S2/S4 still failed, and S4's ceiling
*was* demonstrated (E18b dense: recall 99.3%, passkey 0.725 at a comparable budget) — but it moves the
blame from "the array cannot carry content" to "CE on natural text at 0.5B tokens is the wrong source".

### 7.2 Absolute quality: bits per byte against public models of the same size

Per-token CE in a 128k-vocab tokenizer is not comparable to anything published, so scored as bits per byte
on identical text (32 eval rows, 2048-token prefixes, 267 KB, each model using its own tokenizer;
`/opt/cursor/artifacts/e22_bpb.log`):

| model | params | training tokens | BPB on our rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| E22 arm A | 128M compute / 318M total | **0.44B** | 1.472 |
| E22 arm C | 63M compute | 0.44B | 1.466 |
| pythia-160m | 160M | ~300B (**680×**) | 1.265 |
| SmolLM2-135M | 135M | ~2T (**4500×**) | 0.989 |

Chinchilla-optimal for 128M is ≈ 2.6B tokens, so E22 ran at ~17% of compute-optimal and far from
converged; the D^−0.28 data term alone accounts for roughly 0.4–0.5 BPB between 0.44B and 300B tokens,
which covers the whole 0.21 BPB gap to pythia-160m. Our eval rows are PG-19 books / long PDFs, mildly
out-of-domain for both public models, and our decoder sees only a 1024-token raw segment where they see
2048 — both favour us slightly. **Reading:** absolute quality is unremarkable but exactly where the data
budget puts it; nothing here indicts the architecture. Equally, **no public checkpoint is a valid baseline
for the question E22 asked** — all are trained 2–3 orders of magnitude longer and none reports a
far-context marginal — so matched internal controls (arm C, the dense arm) *are* the measurement
apparatus. Losing E22's dense control cost us S3 outright.

### 7.3 Concept vs dense: the gap is raw *mid-range* reach, not the array

The dense control is `perceiver_ar` with `par_mode="dense"`: 18 full-causal layers, same width
(d 768, 6 q-heads × 128, 2 kv-heads), same data, optimizer, batch and tokens — every token attends to
every earlier token at every layer. Our arms replace that with a 1024-token segment (no chaining across
segments) plus, in arm A, cross-attention to the array. At step 320, the only point where all three exist:

| arm | eval loss @320 | vs dense | unbounded state per token |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense, 18 × full causal | **4.616** | — | 18 layers × 2 kv × 128 × 2(KV) × 2 B = **18.4 KB** |
| E22 arm C (segment-only, 63M) | 4.682 | +1.43% | 0 (segment KV only, 8.4 MB fixed) |
| E22 arm A (the bet, 128M) | 4.691 | +1.6% | array **96 B** (z only); ≈ 0.9 KB if latent + cross-attn KV are also cached |

**Why dense is ahead by that much — and only that much.** Not far context: E18's reach ablation prices the
dense model's *own* raw access at +0.035 nats for keys 2k–8k back (64 mixed rows), +0.105 for reach
512 → 8k, and *negative* beyond its training length. The dense − arm C gap of 0.066 nats sits inside that
band. The 1.4% is the price of losing **raw, exact access at roughly 1k–8k**, which is where natural-text
next-token prediction still has real signal — not the price of losing 8k–32k memory, which is worth
almost nothing at this scale. The same law holds across three experiments, and the gap tracks raw reach:

| geometry | raw chained reach for the predicting stack | gap vs matched dense |
|---|---|---|
| E18 stage A: 12 × SWA-2048 | ≈ 24.6k ≥ context (8k) | **+0.1%** (3.790 vs 3.786 @1.0B) |
| E18 arms A/C: 13 × SWA-256 | ≈ 3.1k in an 8k context | **+2.2%** (4.09 vs ≈ 4.00 @0.5B) |
| E22 arms A/C: 8 × segment-1024, no chaining | 1024 in a 32k context | **+1.4–1.6%** (4.68/4.69 vs 4.616 @320) |

**Why the array does not close the gap although it covers exactly that range.** Per-bucket, arm A beats
arm C only in [1k,4k), [4k,8k) and [8k,16k), by **0.005** nats — while depending on its far slots for
**0.05** and paying **0.054** in [0,1k), where the two arms have *identical* raw access. So the array's
net contribution is ~0.005 nats for 65M extra compute parameters, and arm A partly pays for it by being a
worse local model: routing modelling through a lossy 16:1 pooled detour costs more in the first segment
than the channel returns later. "Used but not useful" (E18's law) with the mechanism now visible.

**Caveat on the number:** step 320 is early (all three later reach ≈ 4.17) and the dense run died at ≈ 390,
so 1.4–1.6% is one snapshot, not a converged compression price. S3 stays unmeasured.

### 7.4 Why the slots are smooth: the pooling ratio is not the compression ratio

r = 16 describes how many slots exist, not how much each slot summarises. Slot j pools 16 encoder states,
but each of those states has a receptive field of 6 × 511 ≈ 3066 tokens through the chained SWA-512
encoder, so slot j's effective view is ≈ `[16j − 3066, 16j + 15]` — **a 3k-token blurred summary placed at
position 16j, not a crisp 16-token chunk**. Consequences, all matching the measurements:

- adjacent slots overlap ≈ 99.5% in receptive field → adjacent cosine **0.69**;
- slots ≥ 192 apart (3072 tokens) have disjoint fields → cosine falls to **0.57** at 8k separation and
  plateaus at the mean pairwise **0.56**, i.e. the shared document direction. The array's correlation
  length is ≈ 200 slots, which is exactly the encoder's receptive field in slot units;
- the "0.17 nats of document embedding" is then unsurprising: with a ~3k-token blur, a slot's content is
  dominated by what the whole neighbourhood is about, and neighbourhoods are highly redundant.

**Design consequence for E23 (a knob the spec did not name):** if slots are to be content-*addressable*,
the encoder's receptive field should be commensurate with the pooling block, not 190× larger — e.g. a
block-local final encoder layer, or window ≈ 64–256 with the last layer confined to the block. This
trades against LCLM's "encoder window ≥ 256" finding, so it is a genuine open question, not a fix:
register it as arm A1 of E23 (same objective, encoder's last two layers block-local) if K1 fires.

### 7.5 Where the compute saving actually comes from — and why it is a constant, not an asymptote

`analysis/geometry_cost_model.py` (added with this note) prices both families analytically. Two results
matter, and the second one changes what the family can promise at 1M/10M.

**Where the saving comes from.** In the dense control all 18 layers are O(S²) and attention is 80% of the
forward FLOPs at 32k, 99.9% at 10M. The concept geometry never lets a layer see the raw sequence globally:
the 6 encoder layers are windowed (512), the 8 decoder self-attentions are segment-confined (1024) — both
linear in S — and the only global mixing happens over 16× fewer items. Per-token work (projections +
SwiGLU) is essentially unchanged, 1.14× cheaper, because arm A still runs 14 token-resolution layers; the
saving is entirely in the attention term. Decode state is the cleaner win and it *is* structural:
18 KB/token of KV cache versus 96 B/token of array (192×), i.e. 180 GB versus 0.96 GB at 10M tokens.

**Why it saturates.** The read is a *dense* read: every token scores every visible slot, so cross-attention
is O(S · S/r) — still quadratic, with the constant divided by r. It is 94% of arm A's FLOPs at 10M, and the
whole-model saving converges to `r · L_dense / L_dec = 16 · 18/8 = 36×` no matter how long the context is.

| geometry at S = 10M | forward FLOPs | vs dense | dominant term | ceiling formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E22 as built (dense read, full-causal latent) | 89.4 P | **34×** | cross-attn, 94% | `r · L_dense/L_dec` = 36× |
| + selective top-64 slot read | 4.97 P | **612×** | latent stack, 53% | `r² · L_dense/L_latent` = 1152× |
| + windowed latent stack (4096 slots) | 2.36 P | **1288×** | per-token MLP, 88% | linear in S; grows with S |

So E22's architecture buys a 34× constant and a 192× state reduction — real, but *not* the sub-quadratic
scaling the 1M/10M goal needs. Getting there requires two further changes the current family does not have:
a **selective read** (top-k slot retrieval instead of attending to all slots) and a **non-global latent
stack** (windowed or hierarchical). Only after both does the model become per-token-work bound, which is
the regime where 10M context is actually affordable. Note this is an argument about cost, not quality:
E22 showed the dense read already fails to *use* far slots, so making the read selective is plausibly
aligned with making it work — a selective read has to name what it wants, which is exactly the
addressability E23 is trying to force. Record as a design constraint for E23's successor, not for E23
itself (E23 must stay one bet).

### 7.6 What the pilot eval tier should stop measuring

At 0.44B tokens the lm-eval 0-shot suite is chance-level noise: hellaswag 0.250 (chance 0.25), winogrande
0.495 (0.5), piqa 0.531 (0.5), arc_easy 0.292 (0.25), commonsense_qa 0.196 (0.2), lambada 0.009, and
`avg_acc` 0.298 (A) vs 0.305 (C) — the control "wins". It costs ~1 GPU-h per checkpoint and cannot
discriminate. Keep it out of pilot gates until ≥ 10B tokens; keep Tier 0 health, the paired ablations and
the dense-label synthetic tasks, and report BPB. (Landed in the `experiment-evaluate` skill,
interpretation rule 0.)
